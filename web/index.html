de_nuke, as a power plant

Counter-Strike 2's de_nuke with everything that is not plant taken away — the pipework, ducting, vessels, machinery, instrumentation and switchyard, in the positions they occupy in the game — inside a ghosted shell of the building itself, so you can see where everything sits.

The scene is about 19 MB and is downloaded once. It is untextured on purpose: every surface is a flat colour that says what the geometry is, not what it looks like in the game.

On a phone this is a viewer and nothing more. The panel, the key legend and the equipment names are all switched off — they are sized for a screen twenty times the area. You get the plant and the means to fly around it. Open it on a laptop to name what you are looking at and to see photographs of the real equipment.

This browser has no WebGPU

The viewer needs it and there is no fallback to offer. Per-instance transforms and colours are read from a storage buffer, and WebGL2 has no storage buffers at all, so it is not a matter of a slower path — the renderer cannot be expressed.

Chrome or Edge is the reliable answer, on Windows and on a Mac whatever version of macOS you are running. Safari has it from Safari 26 (macOS Tahoe) onwards. Firefox has it on Windows from 141 and on macOS later, so it is the least dependable of the three. On Linux Chrome you may need chrome://flags/#enable-unsafe-webgpu.

A desktop build with the same features, which uses Vulkan, Metal or D3D12 and reads the map straight out of your own CS2 install, is in the repository.
